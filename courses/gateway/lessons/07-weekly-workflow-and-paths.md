# Lesson 7. One weekly task, on paper

**Status:** Written draft, 7 November 2026. Not recorded. Not piloted. Not published.

**Objective:** Map one real weekly task as trigger, input, action, human approval, and error handling, then optionally note whether a later video, website, or automation lesson would interest you.

**You finish with:** A weekly task map and the personal completion checklist. An interest note is optional. Nothing is connected, sent, bought, or filed by a tool.

**Pace:** One sitting. Not tied to a calendar day.

## Spoken narration

You have a card, prompts, a brief, three drafts, a source log, and a do-not-paste list. This last lesson puts one repeating task on paper so you can see the human in the loop. We will not build the automation. We will not buy a tool. We will not organise your real files. The map is the practice.

Pick a task that already happens. Sam's fictional task is a Monday list: which sample shop folder is still missing. Yours might be a weekly status note, a batch of captions, or a reminder to yourself about the book swap. If you do not repeat any task, map the next time you will write your three drafts again. That is allowed.

Use five boxes. Trigger: what tells you to start. For Sam, it is Monday morning, not a software ping we switched on. Input: what you gather with your own hands. The sample folder labels, your brief, last week's note. Action: what you do with the chat tool, written as a sentence. "Ask for a six-line list from these labels only." Human approval: who reads it before it leaves the desk. Sam. Error handling: what you do when the tool is down, the draft names a fourth shop, or a folder is missing. You stop, you fix the label, or you skip the week. You do not let a tool email Shop A.

Draw the boxes on the worksheet. Then write one line under the map: "I did not connect this to email, files, or a shop. I did not spend money." If that line is false, disconnect whatever you connected before you finish the lesson. This course does not include live automation.

The second half is a door, not a push. Three later topics exist as ideas in the project notes. They are not courses you can buy from this draft, and they may never be scheduled. Video: how to make a short, honest product or creator clip without a fake customer. Website: how to plan and publish a small site. Automation: how to document a repeating task more fully, still with a person approving each side effect. You may tick one, tick none, or write "not yet". A tick is an interest note. It does not enrol you, charge you, or promise income, views, or saved hours. There is no earnings claim in this course.

If a community exists when you study, you are welcome to say what you made. You are also welcome to stay quiet. Community benefits are not defined in this draft. If a form asks for marketing email, that question is separate from an account and separate from the community. You can finish every lesson without agreeing to marketing. If the form does not exist, ignore this paragraph and keep your toolkit on your own device.

Read the completion checklist. It asks whether each artifact is present, whether lesson 5 has your initials on real pages, and whether the email is still unsent. Then answer the five questions in the knowledge check. The questions are there so you can see your own gaps. They are not a certificate and not a job test.

You started as someone who had heard the words and wanted a practical use. You should now be able to say what the tool does, give it context, keep a brief, edit three drafts, check a claim against a page you opened, refuse a secret and a fake speaker, and show one weekly task that waits for a person. That is the course. The next hour of work is yours.

## Presenter and demo steps

1. Draw five empty boxes. Fill them for Sam. Do not open an automation product.
2. Write the line "I did not connect this" and tick it.
3. Show the three later topics as text, with a spoken line: "Not on sale in this recording. Interest is optional."
4. Show marketing consent as a separate unticked box if you show a mock form. Do not require the tick. Label the mock form "not a live system".
5. Point to the knowledge check. Do not call it a grade.

## Worked example (fictional)

**Label:** Fictional. Not connected. Not sent. No fee.

| Box | Sam's map |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Monday morning on my own calendar. No tool is allowed to start this. |
| Input | My brief, and the labels Shop A, Shop B, Shop C. No real files. |
| Action | Ask the chat tool for a six-line list of which label is still missing, using only notes I typed. |
| Human approval | I read the list before it leaves my notebook. Nobody else is emailed. |
| Error handling | If a fourth shop appears, I delete it. If the tool is down, I write the list myself. If I am unsure, I do not send anything. |

Interest note: "Not yet. I may look at a writing-and-video lesson later if one exists. I am not promised a result."

End with: "This is a paper map. Do not automate it."

## Common mistakes

- Drawing a map and then turning on an integration "to see if it works".
- Putting "the tool approves" in the human box.
- Treating a tick on video, website, or automation as a purchase or a promise.
- Agreeing to marketing email because the form sat next to the lesson button. Those are different choices.
