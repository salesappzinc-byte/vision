# Lesson 5. Sources you opened yourself

**Status:** Written draft, desk-edited 7 November 2026. Not recorded. Not piloted. Not published.

**Objective:** Summarise only from pages you opened, then check each important claim yourself. The tool does not get to mark the summary true.

**You finish with:** A claim-and-source log in the toolkit. Every source line has a title and address you copied from a page you opened, plus your own check mark.

**Pace:** One sitting. Not tied to a calendar day.

## Spoken narration

Drafting is fast. Checking is the part people skip, and it is the part this lesson will not skip. A chat tool can summarise words you give it. It can also invent a study, a date, or a web address. Asking it "are you sure?" and receiving "yes" is not a check. That is the tool agreeing with itself.

You need two pages you opened yourself. One page was checked for this draft on 7 November 2026: `https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:About`. It showed: "Wikipedia is a free online encyclopedia that anyone can edit, and millions already have." Open that address. If you still see that sentence, you may paste it. If the words have changed, copy a sentence you can see. Do not copy counts from memory. This lesson prints none. If the page will not load, do not invent an address.

For the second page, and for both pages if the first will not load: open your browser, find a public page you can read, such as a library events page or a help page you already use, and copy the title and address from the address bar. Do not take an address from the chat tool. Do not paste a private message. Sam's sentence "Shop A is still on the desk" needs no page. It is a label, not a fact about shops. Nia uses a public page only.

When this is recorded, the presenter opens that Wikipedia page, or another live page if it fails, and copies the address from the address bar.

The steps are strict. First, open the page and read the part you care about. Second, copy the title and the full address from the browser, not from the chat tool. Third, paste a short excerpt into the chat, or describe the excerpt in your own words and say it is yours. Fourth, ask for a summary that uses only that text and that lists claims in separate lines. Fifth, for each claim, go back to the open page and mark one of three results: matches, does not match, or not on the page. Sixth, sign the log with your name or initials and the date. The tool does not sign it.

If the summary adds a number you cannot find on the page, the mark is "not on the page". If the summary says a shop saved money, and the page is a software help page that never said that, the mark is "does not match". Leave the claim out of anything you might publish. You do not need a clever argument. You need the page.

Your own plan does not need a web page. "I am writing a list of practice folders" is Sam's plan. "I am planning a book swap" is Nia's plan. A claim about what most people do, or about a study, needs a page you opened or it is deleted.

Personal path: the same log. Claim, source title, address you copied, what the page says in your words, match or not, your initials. No neighbour's private message counts as a source unless they wrote it for you to use. Do not paste their message into the tool for this exercise. Use a public page.

If you cannot open two pages today, stop. The artifact is incomplete, and that is an honest result. Do not fill the log with addresses the tool suggested. Come back when you can open the pages. The rest of the course can wait. It is self-paced.

## Presenter and demo steps

1. Say aloud: one practice address is printed in the lesson, and it was opened on 7 November 2026. Any other address must come from the browser bar.
2. Open `https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:About`. If the checked sentence is still visible, paste only that sentence. If not, paste a sentence you see. Do not read out visitor numbers from memory.
3. Copy one short paragraph from that page into the prompt. Ask for a claim list.
4. Find one claim that is not in the paragraph. Mark it "not on the page".
5. Initial the row. Say: the model does not get a "verified" button.
6. If the page fails to load, switch to a second real page on camera. Do not invent a substitute address.

## Copy-ready prompt

Paste this only after you have pasted text from a page you opened. Do not ask the tool to browse on faith, and do not ask it to confirm its own earlier answer.

```
Summarise only the source text I paste below. Do not add facts from memory.
List each factual claim as its own line.
If the text does not support a claim, do not include it.
Do not write the word verified. Do not give me a web address unless it appears inside the source text I pasted.
I will check your lines against the open page myself.

SOURCE TITLE (I copied this):
SOURCE ADDRESS (I copied this from my browser):
SOURCE TEXT:
[paste a short excerpt you copied from the open page]
```

After you have marked the log, you may use this follow-up. It is still not a certification.

```
I marked these claims as not on the page: [paste them].
Remove them from the summary. Do not replace them with new facts.
```

## Your exercise

1. Open the Wikipedia page named above, or a page you found with the selection steps if it will not load. Open a second page with the selection steps. Copy each title and address from the browser yourself.
2. Produce a short summary from pasted text, not from a vague question.
3. Log at least three claims. At least one mark should be "not on the page" or "does not match" if you find one. If every line matches, say so, and still initial the rows.
4. Do not ask the tool to mark the log correct. Sign it yourself.

## Artifact

Claim-and-source log with your check marks and no tool-authored "verified" stamp.

## Self-check

- Every address was copied from a page I opened.
- I can show one claim I rejected or rewrote after looking.
- I did not treat "yes, that is accurate" from the tool as evidence.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the tool suggest sources and pasting those addresses without opening them.
- Filling the log with an address you did not open. The Wikipedia address counts only if you opened it. A made-up address does not count.
- Summarising a topic you did not paste.
- Hiding a failed check because the summary "sounded right".
