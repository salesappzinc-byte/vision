# Lesson 6. What stays in your hands

**Status:** Written draft, 7 November 2026. Not recorded. Not piloted. Not published.

**Objective:** Decide what you will not paste into a tool, and when a human must approve a draft before it is sent, posted, or used as if a real person said it.

**You finish with:** A do-not-paste list in the toolkit, applied once to your brief and once to your three drafts.

**Pace:** One sitting. Not tied to a calendar day. This lesson is a practical habit. It is not legal advice for every country. Rules differ. When you are unsure, do not publish.

## Spoken narration

You have drafts and a log. This lesson is the gate in front of other people. A chat tool will hold whatever you type. Treat that box as a place you would not leave a house key.

Do not paste passwords, one-time codes, identity numbers, full bank details, or medical notes. Do not paste a real client's books, a private contract, or a neighbour's message. Do not paste a photo of a real person so the tool can imitate them. Our class names, Shop A and the book swap, are safe because they are labels. Your real names are a different decision, and this lesson's decision is no.

Errors survive a nice tone. Look again at names, dates, and steps. If the email says Friday and your brief says the day is not stated, the email is wrong. Bias is a quieter error. If every example is the same kind of shop, the same kind of family, or the same kind of body, ask who is missing. You do not have to write a lecture. You do have to notice when a draft talks as if one kind of person is the only kind.

Rights means you do not upload work you do not have permission to use. That includes other people's articles, photos, music, and logos. Paste a short excerpt you copied for study if the page allows you to use it that way, and keep it inside the fact-check log. Do not ask the tool to reproduce a whole chapter. Likeness means a real person's face or voice. Do not have a tool speak in a customer's voice. Do not build a picture of a real neighbour "as a joke". Fictional Sam has no face you need to clone. Leave it that way.

Disclosure is how you avoid a fake testimonial. If you wrote a post with a tool, you can say "I drafted this with a chat tool and edited it." You must not publish the tool's sentence as if Shop A said it. You must not show a screenshot of earnings, stars, or a saved hour. This course has none to show. If a viewer could think a customer is speaking, rewrite until it is clearly you.

Human approval is the last step, and it is a person. You, or someone you asked, reads the final before it goes anywhere. The tool is not the approver. An automatic send is out of scope for this course. Do not turn on a feature that sends mail, posts, buys, or moves files. If a screen offers that, leave it off.

Apply the list twice. First, read your brief. If a line has a real client's name, replace it now. Second, read the post, the email, and the description. Tick each one: no secret, no fake speaker, no unapproved face, a human still has to approve. Write the ticks on the do-not-paste page so they are not only in your head.

Personal path: the book swap email to a friend is still not sent. A photo of a neighbour's child is not a fun test image. A public library page you opened in lesson 5 can be named. A private chat with the librarian cannot be pasted.

If this feels strict, that is the point of a first course. You can loosen a rule later when you know why it exists. You cannot un-paste a secret.

## Presenter and demo steps

1. Show the do-not-paste headings. Say this is not a complete law lecture.
2. Hold up Sam's email. Point to "Practice copy. Do not send." Do not click send.
3. Cross out a sentence that sounds like a customer quote. Replace it with "This note is my draft."
4. Show a switched-off automation or "send" control if the tool has one. Leave it off. If the tool has no such button, say so, and still say we do not connect anything.
5. Tick the brief and one draft on the checklist, slowly.

## Worked example (fictional)

**Label:** Fictional. Nothing sent.

Sam's do-not-paste lines:

> I will not paste real shop names, receipt photos, tax numbers, or bank details.
> I will not paste a client's spreadsheet.
> I will not ask for a customer's face or voice.
> I will not publish a sentence as if Shop A wrote it.
> A human, me, reads the final before any send. Today the send does not happen.

One edit on the post: delete "Shop owners say this saved their week." Replace with "I drafted this note myself with a chat tool, and I edited it."

## Copy-ready prompt

Use this on your own draft. Do not paste secrets into it.

```
Read the draft below. Do not rewrite it yet.
List: possible secrets, invented quotes, claims of savings or popularity, and any request to copy a real person's face or voice.
Then propose a shorter draft that removes those items.
Do not say the draft is approved. End with "A person still needs to approve this. Do not send or post."

DRAFT:
[paste a draft that contains only sample or your own non-secret text]
```

## Your exercise

1. Write at least six personal do-not-paste lines. Use the toolkit headings so you do not skip likeness or disclosure.
2. Run the prompt on one of your lesson 4 drafts, or do the list by hand.
3. Edit that draft. Keep the "not sent" line.
4. Tick the brief and all three drafts on the checklist.

## Artifact

Do-not-paste list, plus ticks showing you applied it.

## Self-check

- I named secrets I will not paste, in words that fit my life.
- My public-shaped draft does not speak as a customer.
- I can point to the human who approves. It is not the chat tool.

## Common mistakes

- Pasting a real spreadsheet "only this once".
- A disclosure line hidden where nobody will see it, while the headline still reads like a review.
- Turning on auto-send to save time.
- Treating this lesson as permission to ignore local rules. If you are unsure, do not publish.
