# Course research and a proposed build roadmap

Research date: 7 November 2026. This note is for the `salesappzinc-byte/vision` fork. It is research and a build recommendation. It is not course copy, not a tech stack, and not a decision the founder has confirmed.

[VISION.md](../VISION.md) remains the product direction: people who have heard of AI and still need who, why, how, and what, plus what it can do for their own goals; a free gateway; email signup with community membership; then deeper paid courses and relevant affiliate tools. This note does not narrow that vision. The launch segment and the paid-course order below are **proposals for confirmation**.

Nothing here is implemented. Course tuition, a learner's own tool subscription, and any in-site API spend are three different costs. No tuition is proposed, because no price has been verified as ours.

A separate proposal, not part of the gateway build below, is that an approved member can upload a course and earn when a learner buys it. That proposal is in [creator-marketplace-research.md](creator-marketplace-research.md). It does not change the gateway, the three proposed paid paths, or the first-build acceptance criteria in this file. Creator earnings, a course-referral commission, and an external-tool affiliate sale stay different relationships.

## 1. Recommendation

**Proposed launch segment, not a confirmed choice.** Start the free course with non-technical solo business owners, freelancers, and early creators who want AI to save time or make a marketing asset. The founder audience is wider: anyone who has heard of AI and does not yet understand it. A narrower first segment is a hypothesis. It makes the exercises concrete, makes a paid next step easier to see, and matches the tool types the founder already named (video, websites, automation). It is not evidence that this group will pay.

**Free gateway, proposed.** Seven short lessons, about one sitting each, ending in a Personal AI Toolkit the learner can save: a plain-language explanation, 10 prompts, a one-page brief, three edited drafts, a human fact-check log, a "do not paste" list, and one mapped weekly task.

**Proposed paid order, not a confirmed choice.**

1. AI content and realistic, UGC-style product video, taught without fake customers.
2. Build a small website with AI (plan, brand, build, launch checklist).
3. Automate one repeating task, including a file-organisation exercise.

**Later, still in the founder catalog.** AI coding, platforms and services, affiliate opportunities and programs, authorised purchasing automation, AI Facebook ads and related marketing, file organisation if it is not absorbed by the automation course, creating bots and their stack, and AI phone, email, SMS, WhatsApp, and other communications. Purchasing automation stays the learner's own authorised use. Bypassing a service's controls, and unsolicited bulk messaging, stay out.

## 3. Evidence

### 3.1 What the surveys support

These are workforce surveys, mostly United States, plus one United States and United Kingdom study. They support a training gap and a tilt toward practical writing and research tasks. They do not say which course a novice will pay for. They do not cover Singapore or any other market. Target geography is still open. No keyword volumes, conversion rates, or market sizes were found, and none are invented here.

**S1. ZipRecruiter, *Better Work, More to Learn: The 2026 AI Worker Report*.** https://www.ziprecruiter-research.org/economic-insights-research/2026-ai-worker-report. Page text read 7 November 2026. The page is dated Oct 26 under the 2026 title. Survey: 8-21 September 2026, PureSpectrum panel, 1,500+ verified US workers.

- Key findings: job postings that call for using AI grew 159% year over year. 28.9% say they receive enough training. 37.2% are learning the tools on their own.
- The body also says 62.8% receive any form of AI training, while 28.9% receive extensive training, and 37.2% learn entirely on their own because the employer provides none. "Enough" in the key findings and "extensive" in the body are not the same sentence. Both are quoted rather than merged.
- Most often used for research and information gathering (52.1%), summarizing documents (41.6%), and drafting and editing text (40.9%).
- Configuring an existing tool or plugin (36.1%) was more common than building an agent. 31.6% had not built or customized any AI workflow.

**S2. Google/Ipsos, *AI Works for America*.** https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/googleipsos-ai-works-america-poll. Page text read 7 November 2026. Ipsos post dated 19 February 2026. Employee sample: KnowledgePanel, 4,464 employed US adults 18-75, fielded 5-21 December 2025. Margin of sampling error plus or minus 1.3 points at 95% for the full employee sample.

- The lede says practical use cases are necessary for employees to see the relevance of AI in their job tasks.
- 65% have some interest in formal training on using AI at work. 14% say their organization offered AI-related training in the last 12 months.
- 40% say they use AI at work. Less than one in five (18%) are highly confident they are getting the most out of it.
- Reported top uses are writing and learning, not the task split in S1.

**S3. Cornerstone, *Mind the Gap*, press release 20 May 2026.** https://www.cornerstoneondemand.com/company/news-room/press-releases/mind-the-gap-new-cornerstone-ai-skills-study-reveals-half-the-workforce-is-winging-it-to-learn-ai-due-to-low-employer-readiness/. Page text read 7 November 2026. Censuswide survey of 2,000 employed adults, US n=1,000 and UK n=1,000, April 2026. The release says AI-specific questions excluded people who indicated AI is not used in their role (US n=823, UK n=734). Treat the percentages as the release states them, not as a clean share of all adults.

- 14% are open to building AI skills but unsure where to start.
- 32% express negative feelings such as uncertainty, anxiety, resistance, or unfairness.
- Workers' practical definition of an AI skill, as reported: completing tasks faster 30%, applying job-specific tools 26%, writing effective prompts 26%. Critical evaluation of output is lower in that list (22%).

**S4. Express Employment Professionals, newsroom, 22 April 2026.** https://www.expresspros.com/newsroom/news-releases/news-releases/2026/04/8-in-10-employees-say-they-need-ai-training-after-their-companies-already-rolled-out-the-tools. Page text read 7 November 2026. This is no longer a snippet. Harris Poll for Express: 1,003 US adults 18+, 7-20 November 2025 (Job Seeker Report); 1,002 US hiring decision-makers, 3-19 November 2025.

- 75% of job seekers say they are likely to seek additional training in response to AI advancements. 31% say they are very likely.
- A separate figure on the same page: 83% of US job seekers say companies need to formally train employees on AI rather than expecting them to learn on their own. The headline's "8 in 10" matches this 83%, not the 75%.

### 3.3 Practice cost, framing, and rules that were actually read

**S8. OpenAI API pricing.** https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing. Page text read 7 November 2026. Text models are priced per 1M tokens, with separate input and output rates, and the table lists many models. On the Standard table, `gpt-5-nano` is listed at $0.05 input and $0.40 output per 1M tokens. `gpt-5-mini` is still listed, at $0.25 input and $2.00 output per 1M tokens. The page does not, in the text read, mark `gpt-5-mini` deprecated. No deprecation date is used. Re-read this page before choosing a model. This note does not choose one.

**S8b. OpenAI spend limits.** https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/spend-limits. Page text read 7 November 2026. A spend alert sends a notification and API traffic continues. A hard spend limit is a separate control: "Enforce a hard limit" must be turned on, and then affected requests can return HTTP 429. The same page says enforcement is not instantaneous, so recorded spend can slightly exceed the configured amount. This is OpenAI's current documentation, not a rule for every provider. An application-enforced quota and spend ceiling is still required. Do not describe a provider budget alert as a hard cap.

**S9. Gemini API pricing.** https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing. Page text read 7 November 2026. The extracted text did not contain "Veo". The third-party Veo per-second estimate is omitted. The page does describe `gemini-omni-1.2-flash` as a video generation and editing model on the paid tier, up to 15 seconds, with video output listed at $12.00 per 1M tokens. A footnote on that page says billing uses 5,792 tokens per second of 720p video, which it equates to approximately $0.07 per second under that standard rate. The same page says that from 3 December 2026, `gemini-omni-1.1-flash` routes to `gemini-omni-1.2-flash`. Treat the figure as Google's own approximation on that date, and re-read it before any video budget. It is an API cost, not course tuition.

**S10. ChatGPT framing check.** `curl -I -L https://chatgpt.com` on 7 November 2026. Response headers were read. The body was not. The response included a `Content-Security-Policy` whose `frame-ancestors` directive was `'self'` plus specific Chrome extension origins. No `X-Frame-Options` header was present in that header dump. For this site, on this date, the header tells browsers not to frame the page from an ordinary third-party origin. It does not show that other consumer AI sites refuse embeds, and it does not show that a provider-supported embed or OAuth flow is impossible. Scripting a learner's consumer login remains out of scope.

**S11. United States, fake testimonials.** 16 CFR 465.2, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465/section-465.2. eCFR text read 7 November 2026. The eCFR is not an official legal edition. Section 465.2 makes it an unfair or deceptive practice for a business to write, create, or sell a consumer review, consumer testimonial, or celebrity testimonial that materially misrepresents that the person exists, that they used or experienced the product, service, or business, or what that experience was. Purchasing or disseminating such a testimonial when the business knew or should have known of the misrepresentation is also covered. This is a United States rule about misrepresented reviews and testimonials. It is not a ban on every AI video, and it is not a statement of law anywhere else.

**S12. United States, material connections.** 16 CFR 255.5, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255/section-255.5. eCFR text read 7 November 2026. When a connection between an endorser and the seller might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and the audience would not reasonably expect it, the connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Example 11 on that page is an affiliate link: if the reviewer is paid a portion of the sale, that compensation should be disclosed because it could affect how visitors weigh the review. This is a United States guide. It is not legal advice and not a rule transcribed for every country.

**S13. Meta, three different scopes.** Pages read 7 November 2026. Do not collapse them into "Meta requires every AI ad to be labeled."

- Organic posts. Community Standards, Misinformation, https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/misinformation/. "We require people to disclose, using our AI-disclosure tool, whenever they post organic content with photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, and we may apply penalties if they fail to do so." The policy page lists several earlier policy dates; the sentence above is what the page showed when read. It names photorealistic video and realistic-sounding audio, not every caption or every image.
- Ads about social issues, elections, or politics. https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/SIEP-advertising/SIEP/. Advertisers must disclose when such an ad contains a photorealistic image or video, or realistic-sounding audio, created or edited with third-party generative AI, in specified cases: a real person saying or doing something they did not; a realistic-looking person who does not exist, a realistic-looking event that did not happen, or altered footage of a real event; or a realistic event that allegedly occurred but is not a true recording. Undisclosed ads can be rejected, and repeated failure may bring penalties. The page says requirements vary by country, that this disclosure feature may not be available in a region, and that these ads are not allowed to run in the European Union except for limited election-authority notices.
- Detection on ads, which is not the same as an advertiser's duty to disclose every ad. The SIEP page says that beginning 1 June 2026 Meta also uses automated detection, and when it detects third-party generative AI an "AI Info" label appears in "About this Ad". Meta's news post, updated 1 June 2026, https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/, describes the same "About this ad" destination and says "AI info" labels already applied to ads created or significantly edited with Meta's own generative AI creative tools also live there. The midterms post, https://about.fb.com/news/2026/02/meta-prepares-for-2026-us-midterms/, says advertisers are still required to self-disclose when they use AI to create or edit ads about social issues, elections, or politics "in certain cases." The cases read in the SIEP policy are the ones quoted above.

## 4. How the recommendation follows the evidence

The surveys point at practical use, especially writing, summarizing, and research, and at people who are unsure where to start. That supports a short gateway whose artifacts are a brief, drafts, and a checked summary, not a theory course. Google's Coursera specialization already occupies the generalist intro, with a very large displayed enrollment (S5). A free gateway that only defines AI will resemble that supply. The proposed difference is a saved toolkit tied to the learner's own goal, then a bridge into a specific build.

The paid order is judgement.

- **Video first, as a proposal.** A finished clip is easy to show and matches the founder's ultra-realistic video, UGC, and product-ad family. The cost of that choice is upkeep and money. Tools and their screens change, so screen recordings go stale. A learner will often need their own video-tool subscription, separate from tuition. If the site later generates video through an API, S9 shows that at least one current Google video model is billed in output tokens and approximated per second, on the paid tier only. Compliance is also heavier: under S11, it is an unfair or deceptive practice for a business to create a testimonial that materially misrepresents that the person exists or used the product; S13's organic rule is about photorealistic video and realistic audio; S13's advertiser disclosure duty, as read, is for social-issue, election, and political ads in the cases listed, not for every product ad. Teaching product video still has to keep fake customers out.
- **Website second, as a proposal.** A small site is also a visible artifact, and the work is planning, copy, brand, and launch, which the founder already listed. It is a calmer production load than video if the course stays tool-agnostic and uses a dated comparison instead of a single vendor's clicks. Affiliate fit (builders, domains, hosting) is a hypothesis. No vendor is selected. The tradeoff against video is less spectacle and a result that depends on the learner publishing something.
- **Automation third, as a proposal.** It sits closest to the tasks surveys actually report (drafting, summarizing, configuring an existing tool). It can absorb file organisation as one module. It is a weaker first impression than a video, and connecting real accounts raises privacy questions, so the passing artifact cannot depend on the learner handing over private files.
- **Why not coding, Facebook ads, bots, or calls first.** Those remain founder families. Coding and bots ask for more technical comfort than the gateway's novice. Facebook ads add media spend and a policy surface that changes, and this research does not support promising ad results. Calls, SMS, and WhatsApp add consent and telecom rules that differ by country and were not mapped. Purchasing bots add retailer terms and a high chance of being read as evasion; they stay deferred until a legitimate, authorised scope is written.

The narrow segment is the same kind of judgement. Solo owners, freelancers, and early creators give lesson 3 a real brief and give the paid courses an obvious artifact. The gateway lessons still teach who, why, how, and what in plain language, so a learner outside that segment is not turned away. Whether the segment is right is a question for the validation plan, not a finding.

## 5. Free gateway: seven lessons

**Working title:** AI for your own work, in seven short lessons.

**Proposed audience for this version:** the segment in section 1. A learner outside it can still use the same lessons on a personal goal.

**Prerequisite:** none. No paid AI subscription is required for the gateway if the in-site text practice box exists. If that box is not ready, the lesson must say which outside tool the learner may use, and that this is their own account, separate from course tuition.

**Toolkit the learner finishes with:**

- a short explanation, in their words, of who AI is for, why people use it, how a chat tool produces an answer, and what it is not
- 10 prompts they can reuse
- a one-page brief of their work, creator niche, or personal goal
- three edited drafts: a post, an email, and a short description of something they offer or care about
- a fact-check log on one summary, with the sources they used and their own mark that they checked them
- a list of what they will not paste into a tool
- one weekly task mapped to write, make video, build a site, or automate

| Lesson | Learner outcome | Exercise | Artifact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. What it is | Explain who, why, how, and what in plain language, including that the tool can be wrong and that they stay responsible | Watch a short worked example, then write the explanation without the model | Plain-language card |
| 2. A useful prompt | Write a prompt with a role, a task, context, a format, and an example | Rewrite one weak prompt in the practice box | Start of the 10-prompt library |
| 3. Your brief | Produce a one-page brief a later lesson can reuse | Answer the lesson's questions; edit the draft the practice box returns | One-page brief |
| 4. Three drafts | Turn the brief into three assets and edit them | Generate, compare, and keep the edited versions | Post, email, description |
| 5. Check the work | Compare a model summary with real sources and mark what was wrong | Sources must be supplied in the lesson or by the learner. The learner checks each claim. The product must not mark the summary "verified" because the model agreed with itself | Fact-check log |
| 6. Safe use | Decide what stays out of the tool, and when a piece should say that AI was used | Apply the list to their own brief and drafts | Do-not-paste list |
| 7. What next | Map one real weekly task, and record which later course matches it | A short routing quiz. The result is a suggestion and an interest record, not a promise that the paid course fits | Workflow map and interest record |

**Assessment:** the toolkit checklist. Each artifact is present, and lesson 5 shows sources plus the learner's own check. There is no score that claims the learner is job-ready, and no number for how many people should finish.

**Production assets:** seven lesson scripts, one toolkit template, the routing questions, and captions if video is used. Keep examples tool-agnostic.

**Website requirements for this course:** lesson pages, a text practice box, a place to save the toolkit, email signup, community membership recorded at signup, marketing permission stored separately from the account if that guardrail is accepted, and the interest record from lesson 7. Community benefits stay TBD, as in VISION.md. Exact lesson-gating (what is visible before signup) is a test, not a decision. Do not collect payment on this course.

## 6. Proposed paid paths

Order is a proposal. Each course assumes the gateway toolkit. Tuition is unset. Any external subscription is the learner's cost and must be named before they start. Any in-site generation credit is a third cost, capped by the application, and is not included unless the founder later adds it.

### 6.1 AI content and realistic product video

**Prerequisite:** gateway toolkit; a real product, offer, or creator topic; willingness to use either their own video tool or a capped in-site credit if one exists. No ad-spend requirement.

**Modules:**

1. Hooks and scripts from the learner's brief.
2. Still images and product shots, with a record of what is real and what is generated.
3. Text or image to a short clip. Tool-agnostic, with a dated comparison table.
4. Voice and captions. Consent if the voice or face is a real person's.
5. UGC-style and product ads that do not pretend a customer exists. Use a disclosed AI presenter, or the learner's own footage and their own words. No fake testimonial (S11).
6. Cut for the formats they actually post.
7. Disclosure for the place they will post. Teach the three Meta scopes in S13 separately. Do not tell learners that every Meta ad must be self-labeled.

**Artifact:** a small pack for one product or topic: scripts, one finished short video, a rights and likeness log, and a disclosure note. Length is a teaching choice. 15 to 30 seconds is a reasonable brief, not a measured standard.

**Assessment:** a rubric for a clear hook, an honest description of what the viewer is seeing, a disclosure note, and the rights log. Peer comments in the community are optional. No rubric item for predicted sales or views.

**Production assets:** lesson scripts, prompt and storyboard templates, a dated tools table with affiliate disclosure next to any affiliate link (S12), example packs that use disclosed fiction rather than fake reviews.

**Website requirements:** video player, file or link submission, a gallery if community review is on, and a rights-log form. In-site video generation, if added, is paid-tier API spend (S9 is one example) and needs the application quota in section 7. It is not part of the first build.

### 6.2 Build a website with AI

**Prerequisite:** gateway brief. No coding requirement.

**Modules:**

1. Goal, pages, and copy.
2. Name, palette, and logo concepts, including what an AI logo does not grant (trademark clearance is not covered and is not promised).
3. Choose a way to build: no-code builder or an AI builder. Dated comparison. No required vendor.
4. Build the pages from the plan.
5. A contact or signup form and a privacy notice the learner can edit. The notice is a starter, not legal advice.
6. A basic check: mobile layout, readable text, alt text.
7. Launch checklist: domain, analytics, and a rollback note. Publishing on a free preview is an acceptable finish if a domain would force an extra purchase.

**Artifact:** a 3 to 5 page site on a public URL, or a hosted preview plus the launch checklist.

**Assessment:** the URL or preview loads, the form or its stand-in is visible, a privacy notice is present, and images have alt text. No traffic or revenue claim.

**Production assets:** page templates, a checklist, and short recordings. Budget to re-record when a builder's interface changes. That maintenance is a reason this course is proposed second rather than as a forever-stable product.

**Website requirements:** lesson pages and a submission field for a URL. No site builder has to be embedded.

### 6.3 Automate one repeating task

**Prerequisite:** gateway lesson 7 workflow map.

**Modules:**

1. Pick a task that already repeats.
2. Trigger, action, condition.
3. One recipe: a form or an email becomes a sheet or note, then a short AI summary, then a message to the learner. A person approves before anything is sent onward.
4. Where the model is allowed to draft, and where a human must accept.
5. Organising files the learner chooses to share: rename, summarize, or sort a sample folder. Private files stay optional.
6. What breaks, what the connected account can see, and how to turn it off.

**Artifact:** one working automation with a short write-up, or, if the learner will not connect an account, a written recipe and a dry-run on sample data. Any time log is the learner's own note, not a result the course claims.

**Assessment:** the write-up shows the trigger, the human approval, and what data left their machine. A demo video is welcome and not required.

**Production assets:** one sample recipe, a privacy checklist, a workflow-recipe page that can start with this single recipe.

**Website requirements:** lesson pages and a submission field. No particular automation vendor is embedded.

## 7. In-site practice for the first build

Text only. The server calls a provider API with a key that is not in the browser. Learners do not need their own model account for the gateway exercises.

Supported as a possibility to validate, not as a promise: official OAuth or an embed where that provider's current docs allow it. The ChatGPT header check (S10) is one consumer site refusing ordinary framing. It is not a survey of providers.

**Claim-checking rule:** lesson 5 and any later "check this" exercise accept a completion only when the learner has attached the real sources they used and has marked that a person checked them. The model must not be able to mark its own output verified.

**Cost controls for the first build:**

- Logged-in learners only.
- An application quota: per-person daily request cap, a max output length, and a spend ceiling in the application that stops further calls when the ceiling is reached.
- A provider spend alert may be added. It is not the ceiling. If the chosen provider is OpenAI, S8b says an alert does not stop traffic, and a hard limit stops it only when that control is turned on, with a possible lag. Other providers were not checked.
- Do not store the raw text of lesson 6's "do not paste" examples as a reason to retain sensitive inputs. Retention rules are still an open design choice.
- Video generation is out of the first build.

External tool subscriptions, in-site API spend, and course tuition stay separate line items in any later pricing conversation.

## 8. Guardrails to write into the site copy

These are teaching and product constraints. They are not a legal opinion, and they are not a claim that the same rule exists in every country. Target geography is undecided. Before launch in a country, a qualified person has to check that country's privacy, advertising, and telecom rules. This note does not do that.

- Affiliate links and paid recommendations disclose the connection next to the link. S12 is the United States anchor for why.
- Courses do not teach learners to create fake customer reviews or testimonials. S11 is the United States anchor.
- Likeness and voice of a real person need that person's consent. That is a course rule even where a statute was not reviewed.
- No promised earnings, sales, views, or ad results.
- Account email and marketing email are separate choices. Marketing mail has an unsubscribe path. A privacy explanation exists before marketing mail is sent. This repeats the guardrail already marked for confirmation in VISION.md. It is not legal sufficiency.
- Community membership is created at signup. Benefits remain TBD.
- Communications courses, when they are built, start from consent. Unsolicited bulk messaging is out of scope. Country-by-country calling and messaging rules are not stated here because they were not researched.
- Purchasing automation, when it is reconsidered, is limited to the learner's own authorised use and to the service's terms. Bypassing controls is out of scope.

## 9. Lightweight validation

No target rates. Record what happens, then a person decides. Do not take payment unless the founder explicitly allows a test purchase.

1. A page with the free course and three interest actions: video, website, automation. Store which action a signed-up person chooses.
2. Talk with a small group from the proposed segment, on the order of 8 to 15 conversations. Ask what they already try to do with AI, what they have paid for, and which of the three artifacts they would want next. Write the notes down. Do not convert the notes into a fake demand statistic.
3. Run the gateway with a small beta. Record lesson completion, toolkit saves, and which paid path lesson 7 suggests. The numbers are observations, not success criteria.
4. Only after those notes exist, decide whether to produce paid course 1, to swap the order, or to change the segment. A founding cohort, if any, waits for the founder's approval and for a price the founder sets.

## 10. First-build acceptance criteria

The first build is the gateway and the interest test. It is not the three paid courses in full production.

1. Seven lessons are published, each with an objective, an exercise, and a one-sentence outcome. The toolkit template contains every artifact in section 5.
2. Lesson 5 cannot be completed by the model approving itself. Completion stores the sources and a human-checked flag.
3. Signup stores an email and creates community membership. If the marketing-consent guardrail is accepted, that consent is a separate stored field, and a marketing message is not sent without it. An unsubscribe path exists before any marketing message is sent.
4. The text practice box calls the provider from the server. A logged-out visitor cannot use it. The application stops calls when the per-person quota or the application spend ceiling is hit. A provider alert, if configured, is documented as an alert.
5. Lesson 7 writes an interest record for video, website, or automation, and the person can open a short preview of that proposed course. The preview states that the full course is not produced yet.
6. Public pages exist for affiliate disclosure, a privacy explanation, and an AI-media note. The AI-media note distinguishes S11, organic Meta disclosure, and Meta's social-issue/election/political ad disclosure, and it says other countries are not covered.
7. No tuition is shown. No video-generation credit is enabled. No checkout is enabled.
8. Paid-course scripts and galleries are not required for this acceptance. They wait on section 9.
9. Creator upload, creator review, and creator payout are outside this list. Their proposed checks are in the marketplace note.
